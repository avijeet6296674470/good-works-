# Avijeet Thakur: BANNED WORDS AND PATTERNS

Applies to every string that ships: page copy, titles, descriptions, alt text, FAQ answers, schema text, text inside images, email templates. Enforced by the code guard and the copy gates. Flag a ban rather than working around it.

---

## 1. Hard bans

### Punctuation
**Em dash and en dash. Never, in any shipped string.** Use "to" for ranges, a comma, or a new sentence. Hyphens in compound words are fine.

### Names
- "Abhijeet", "Avijit", "Avijeet Thakkur" and any other variant: always **Avijeet Thakur**. Exception: the email address and URLs.
- "Full Stack" without hyphen as a modifier: write **full-stack**.
- "Wordpress": always **WordPress**. "Woocommerce": always **WooCommerce**. "Open AI": always **OpenAI**. "N8N": always **n8n**.

### Machine-writing vocabulary
delve, leverage (verb), unleash, unlock (metaphor), elevate (metaphor), seamless, streamline, game-changer, revolutionise, transform (metaphor), cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, world-class, next-level, top-notch, robust, holistic, synergy, curated, vibrant, bustling, nestled, "whether you are X or Y", "it is worth noting", "in today's fast-paced world", "look no further", "at the end of the day", "the perfect blend of", "something for everyone", "take your business to the next level", "digital presence" (as filler), "end-to-end solutions", "passionate about", "tailored solutions", "one-stop shop".

### Positioning errors
- "agency" as a self-description, "our team", "we" meaning a company: this is one person. Use "I". ("You and I" for joint work is fine.)
- "guru", "ninja", "rockstar", "expert" as a self-description. Show proof instead. ("SEO Specialist" as a CV job title is allowed when quoted.)
- "cheap", "affordable", "budget" as a selling point.

---

## 2. Search-spam patterns
- A heading that talks about search itself. Headings state a benefit to a human.
- Opening by echoing the query ("Looking for a full-stack developer?").
- Several keyword variants stacked in one sentence, or a city tail repeated in one paragraph.
- Raw keywords as visible chips or eyebrows.
- Keyword lists dressed as content.
- Fabricated social proof: star glyphs, review tiles, testimonial cards for reviews that do not exist.

---

## 3. Unverifiable superlatives
Banned in body copy: best, top-rated, number one, leading, premier, award-winning, renowned, "#1", "unmatched", "unbeatable", "guaranteed results", "100% satisfaction".

Exception: a title or description may carry a mapped keyword phrase that contains an evaluative word, because a title is a search label. Body prose may not.

---

## 4. Register
Plain English, short sentences, grade 8. First person singular. Numbers and nouns over adjectives. No humour on service pages. State the strength, show the proof, move on. Never defensive: do not argue with objections nobody raised.

---

## 5. When a ban gets in the way
Flag it. Rewrite the copy, record an explicit exception here, or discover the fact behind it is unsupported. Do not publish quietly.
